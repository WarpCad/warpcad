## 1. How long a lineage does a real session build? (the lineage-root case)

`bench/fold-benchmark.mjs`'s new core-032 section. An edit at the root of one lineage of N
subtracts (every later row depends on it), and a cold Open of the same records. Node, median
(p95), ms:

| lineage | records | root edit | cold Open |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 subtract | 3 | 0.2 (0.5) | 0.3 (1.2) |
| 2 | 5 | 0.9 (3.4) | 1.0 (1.0) |
| 3 | 7 | 1.6 (4.6) | 1.7 (4.3) |
| 5 | 11 | 3.5 (22.7) | 3.6 (6.6) |
| 10 | 21 | 14.7 (77.6) | 14.8 (15.1) |
| 20 | 41 | 47.7 (376.6) | 49.1 (480.7) |
| 40 | 81 | 281.7 (1078.1) | 201.7 (1429.1) |

The cost is super-linear, because each subtract's result has more polygons than the last.
A root edit fits a 16 ms frame up to about **10** dependent subtracts on one body.

What the sessions this project actually specifies build (`TODO/ACCEPTANCE.md`):

- Scenario 12, the history-editing scenario, builds cube → chamfer (→ fillet) plus
  independent bodies. Measured as a document, a cube / chamfer / sphere / rotate / move log:
  **0.5 ms** root edit, **0.4 ms** Open (a one-off scratch probe, same method).
- Scenarios 4 and 5 are one subtract each. Scenarios 3 and 15 hang one part off a board: a
  camera snapped to a Pi, a cube placed against the Pi's AV jack. Scenarios 1, 13 and 14 are
  independent bodies.
- No scenario and no fixture builds more than a handful of dependent steps on one body.
  That is from reading their step lists, not from instrumenting a driven session.

So the lineage-root edit is **not** worth option 3 on today's evidence. A 20-40-step
single lineage, where it would matter, is synthetic.

## 2. Open, in the browser: where the block is (dev build, split probe)

To decide between moving the fold (option 1) and announcing it (option 2), the one number
that matters is how much of Open's freeze is the fold. A scratch probe on the dev server
timed, in-page, `store.load(entries)` (fold + store refresh, one synchronous call) against
the worst frame gap of the whole Open, until every body's volume had landed. Chromium,
laptop, median of 5, ms:

| scene | CPU | `store.load` (fold + refresh) | worst frame gap, whole Open | fold's share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fillet-50 | 1× | 86.5 | 127.9 | 68 % |
| fillet-50 | 4× | 266.2 | 301.3 | 88 % |
| 24-bodies | 1× | 113.7 | 201.4 | 56 % |
| 24-bodies | 4× | 397.2 | 431.1 | 92 % |
| chain-40 | 1× | 184.8 | 189.4 | 98 % |
| chain-40 | 4× | 642.6 | 660.4 | 97 % |

Option 1 moves the fold, but it has to pay the return of the whole `scene.byId`, measured
again here at **10.5 / 19.2 / 32.4 ms** (fillet-50 / 24-bodies / chain-40, Node). At 4×
that is roughly 40-130 ms. Estimated worst gap at 4× with option 1: the non-fold remainder
plus 4× transport, **≈ 80 / 110 / 150 ms**, down from 301 / 431 / 660 ms. Better, and
still 5-10 frames.

The same option 1 would make **every realistic Open slower**. A scenario-sized document
folds in 0.4-3.5 ms (section 1). A worker round trip plus the transport floor is more
than that.

## 4. In the browser: Open, production build, before and after

Chromium, laptop 1440×900. **Worst frame gap**, from the file being handed to the chooser
until every body is on screen with its volume. **First feedback**, from the same trigger to
the first change in the header's save-status chip or File button. Before this task that was
the `Opened …` notice, after the fold. After it, the painted `Opening …` state. Median
(min-worst), ms:

| scene | CPU | worst gap, before | worst gap, after | first feedback, before | first feedback, after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fillet-50 | 1× | 135.2 (134.9-150.2) | 129.2 (128.5-146.4) | 116 | **9** |
| fillet-50 | 4× | 376.7 (373.8-381.6) | 375.9 (372.8-389.6) | 379 | **11** |
| 24-bodies | 1× | 209.2 (206.9-212.1) | 202.5 (200.4-203.3) | 150 | **8** |
| 24-bodies | 4× | 510.5 (506.5-512.6) | 522.8 (507.1-556.2) | 515 | **8** |
| chain-40 | 1× | 205.4 (194.6-217.4) | 189.5 (187.2-197.5) | 207 | **6** |
| chain-40 | 4× | 673.0 (657.3-716.5) | 730.6 (687.0-764.4) | 676 | **9** |

The freeze is the same, as it was meant to be. Every difference is within the spread,
except chain-40 at 4×, where after runs ~30 ms slower at every percentile. That may be the
extra task the fold now starts from. It is 5 % of a 700 ms block on a synthetic worst case,
and is recorded rather than explained away. What changed is **when the user learns
something is happening**: 116-676 ms before, **6-11 ms** after, whatever the scene and the
throttle.

An earlier run of the same script without the feedback column (before build only) gave the
same gaps: 130.1 / 371.4, 202.4 / 509.1, 199.0 / 681.9 ms at 1× / 4×.

## 5. What was decided

Option 2: an announced pending state, then the same synchronous fold. Neither option for
the lineage case. The reasoning is in `docs/decisions/kernel-evaluation-worker.md`,
slice 4.
