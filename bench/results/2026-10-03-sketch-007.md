# `TODO/sketch-007`: one snap search per pointer event, on a 400-point outline

**Machine**: Apple M4 Pro, macOS 27.0, Node v24.14.1.
**Engine**: plain Node (V8), no browser. The search is framework-free (`src/sketch/snap.js`),
so this measures the search itself, not the editor's SVG repaint.
**Fixture**: a closed, wobbly 400-point outline about 60 mm across (a traced shape), plus a
20 × 10 mm rectangle and an 8 mm circle: 815 point targets, 404 segments, 1 circumference.
Pointer positions sweep across the whole outline; snap radius 12 px at the editor's opening
zoom (≈ 2.2 px/mm, so ≈ 5.5 mm). 200 000 searches per loop.
**Method**: `node --expose-gc --max-semi-space-size=256 bench/snap-search.mjs`, three runs.
Time is batches of 100 searches (p50/p95/p99/worst of the per-search mean). Allocation is
heap growth across one loop with the young generation big enough that nothing is scavenged
mid-loop, measured on each loop's second pass. **Baseline**: the same loop with no search.
**Control**: `snapPoint` without the reused result object, its result kept in a global so
V8 can't optimise the allocation away. The control proves the method can see allocation.

## Found by this benchmark, and fixed: `Math.hypot` allocated on every call

The first version measured distances with `Math.hypot(dx, dy)`. With the reused result object
the loop still grew the heap by ~2.6 B/search, and it did so even for searches that hit
nothing. A scratch copy with `Math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy)` in place of `hypot` grew the heap
by **0 bytes** over 200 000 and 2 000 000 searches. V8's `Math.hypot` is a varargs builtin
and was not inlined here. `snap.js` now uses the explicit form, and says why in its header.

| | per search p50 | p95 | p99 | worst | heap growth / 200 000 searches |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before (`Math.hypot`) | 10.4 µs | 11.3 µs | 11.5 µs | 12.7–32.4 µs | 528 816 B (2.6 B/search) |
| **after** (`Math.sqrt`) | **1.9 µs** | **2.2 µs** | **2.4 µs** | **2.6–3.1 µs** | **19 008 B**, against a 10 640 B no-search baseline |
| control (new result per hit) | — | — | — | — | 922 360 B (4.6 B/search) |

The ~8 KB left over after the baseline does not grow with the number of searches. It is a
fixed cost, not a per-event object. At a 120 Hz pointer, the search takes about 0.2 ms
per second of dragging, well inside the 16 ms frame.

## What this does not cover

The viewport's half of a pointer event: projecting the pointer onto the sketch plane, the
target-cache identity walk (no allocation, and no rebuild during a point drag because the
dragged outline is excluded), and writing the marker's reactive fields that `SketchStrokes.vue`
repaints. Those are the same per-event pattern the Line tool's rubber band already uses
(`TODO/sketch-006`). A browser frame-time run is a candidate follow-up if a traced outline in
the thousands of points ever feels heavy.
