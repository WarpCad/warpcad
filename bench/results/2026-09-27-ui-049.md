# `TODO/ui-049`: the object list's hover highlight, swept down a fully expanded Pi

**Machine**: Apple M4 Pro, macOS 27.0, Node v24.14.1.
**Browser**: Playwright 1.63.0, Chromium, **headless, software rasteriser**. GPU cost is not
represented; every conclusion below is about main-thread work and shader-program churn,
not fill rate on a real GPU.
**Build**: the **production build** (`npm run build && npx vite preview --port 4173`), per
`.claude/prompts/perf-benchmark.md` rule 2.
**Fixture**: a 100 × 70 × 40 case with a Raspberry Pi B+ wholly inside it, loaded through
**File ▾ → Open…**; the Pi's row expanded, so all 23 part rows (13 visible, 10 hidden) are
listed. Viewport 1440 × 1800, so every row is on screen without scrolling.
**Method**: `node bench/hover-sweep.mjs --url http://localhost:4173 --runs 5 --rates 1,4,6`.
A real mouse (`page.mouse.move`, 6 steps between adjacent row centres) sweeps down all 23
part rows and back up; every `requestAnimationFrame` delta during the sweep is recorded.
One discarded warm-up sweep first. **Control**: the identical path shifted onto the Library
column, where moving the pointer changes nothing. CPU throttling via CDP stands in for a
phone. Heap is CDP `Runtime.getHeapUsage` after a forced GC, before and after N more sweeps.

## Found by this benchmark, and fixed: a shader recompile on every hover of a translucent part

The first run (1× CPU, 5 runs) showed the median untouched but a heavy tail:

| sweep | frames | p50 | p95 | p99 | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | 1405 | 16.6 | 18.4 | 18.9 | 23.2 |
| hover, **before fix** | 1385 | 16.7 | 18.7 | **90.0** | **105.7** |

Logging `data-hover` against every frame over 30 ms put all of them on entering and leaving
`mb`, the Pi's alpha-0.75 board. Each paint pass freed cached materials it did not use, so
hovering `mb` disposed its plain translucent material, which was the only live user of that
transparent shader program. The stall is consistent with three.js deleting that program and
recompiling it for the hovered copy on the next frame, and again in reverse on leave, ~90 ms
each way. **Inferred, not CPU-profiled**: only that one translucent part stalled, and keeping
its material alive removed the stall, but no profile names the compile call. Opaque parts never
hit this, because the viewport's shared palette materials keep the opaque program alive.
The fix: `createColorMaterialCache`'s `endPass` keeps a colour's plain and hovered
materials as a pair while either is used (`src/render/mesh.spec.js` pins it).

## After the fix, all three rates

| CPU | sweep | frames | p50 | p95 | p99 | worst | frames > 16.7 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1× | control | 1405 | 16.7 | 18.0 | 18.5 | 21.8 | 607 |
| 1× | hover | 1405 | 16.7 | 18.4 | 19.6 | 32.6 | 687 |
| 4× | control | 1405 | 16.7 | 18.5 | 19.0 | 19.5 | 678 |
| 4× | hover | 1405 | 16.8 | 18.8 | 19.8 | 21.1 | 764 |
| 6× | control | 1405 | 16.7 | 18.5 | 19.1 | 20.2 | 682 |
| 6× | hover | 1405 | 17.1 | 20.7 | 21.7 | 23.5 | 815 |

("Frames > 16.7 ms" is dominated by vsync jitter in both columns: the headless compositor
schedules frames at ~16.7 ± 1 ms, so roughly half of all frames land just over the line
with nothing happening at all, as the control shows.)

- **Change rate, not pointer rate**: every sweep made **272 pointer moves and exactly 90
  hover changes** (a leave and an enter per row crossed, 46 rows each way): the store's
  same-target guard and the row's already-hovered check hold under a real mouse.
- **Change → next frame**: p50 0.2 / 1.1 ms, p95 0.3 / 1.9 ms, worst 1.0 / 3.1 ms at 1× / 6×.
- **Tail against control**: the hover sweep's p99 sits 1.1 ms (1×), 0.8 ms (4×) and 2.6 ms
  (6×) above the control's, and its worst frame is one 32.6 ms frame in 1405 at 1×.

## Heap: not a leak

After a forced GC, the heap grew by the same amount in every run (332 ± 1 KB after 10 more
sweeps, all 15 runs). Varying the sweep count on one run:

| more sweeps | heap delta after GC |
|---|---|
| 3 | 154 KB |
| 10 | 332 KB |
| 20 | 359 KB |

It plateaus: +27 KB between 10 and 20 sweeps, against +178 KB between 3 and 10. That is the
shape of warm caches (JIT, three.js program/material bookkeeping), not per-hover
retention. The per-hover objects (twins, the transient hidden-part mesh and its geometry,
the hovered material) are disposed on every change, and the unit specs pin each of those.

## Re-run first after any change here

`node bench/hover-sweep.mjs --url http://localhost:4173 --runs 5 --rates 1` and read the
hover row's **p99 and worst**. A shader-program churn regression shows up there as ~90 ms
frames long before it moves the median.
