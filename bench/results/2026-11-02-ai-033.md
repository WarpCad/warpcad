# `TODO/ai-033`: how one AI turn scales with its step count

**Question.** The project's AI step limit replaces the constant 200 records per turn, with a
default of 1,000 and a ceiling of 5,000 (owner decision 2026-11-02). Does a 5,000-record turn
leave the app unresponsive? If it does, the ceiling comes down to what was measured.

**Machine**: Apple M4 Pro, Darwin 27.0.0, Node v24.22.0. Plain Node with no browser and no CPU
throttling, so a phone runs these several times slower. Main-thread work only: the trial fold
runs inside every `appendAll`.
**Command**: `node bench/fold-benchmark.mjs --ai-turns --runs 5 --ai-sizes 200,1000,2000,5000`
**Fixture**: the turn is `data-004`'s `filletScene` mix (a cuboid, two moves and a rim fillet per
body, so 5,000 records is 1,250 bodies), landed as 50-record calls on top of a 200-record project
of the user's own (the same mix, ids prefixed `user-`).

- **live call**: one 50-record call through the real tab endpoint (`createTabEndpoint`, the
  path WebMCP and the live bridge take), with a one-tool stub registry that appends exactly as
  the real append tools do. The value is the median call of the turn, then its worst call.
- **live Undo AI turn**: `endpoint.undoLast()` on that group.
- **fork call / chat landing / chat Undo AI turn**: the BYOK chat. The fork builds the turn,
  `live.mergeFrom(fork.mergeState())` lands it, and `planMergeUndo` + `applyMergeUndo` takes it
  back.

The per-call change costs nothing measurable: every after figure is within the before run's spread.

## Verdict: the ceiling stays at 5,000

- **WebMCP and the live bridge** pay per call, and the cost per call grows slowly: a median of
  32-35 ms at 5,000 records against 21 ms at 200, and 59-62 ms for the worst call of the turn.
  The turn's total (3.5 s) is spread across 100 calls with the agent's own round trip between
  each one, so the app never stops responding for longer than one call. That is two to four
  frames, the same order as one boolean today.
- **The BYOK chat** pays once, at landing: 554 ms at 5,000 records. But it **cannot reach**
  5,000, because 40 tool calls of at most 50 records is 2,000 (`stepsPerTurn` × `recordsPerCall`,
  which stay constants). The largest landing it can make is 2,000 records, 81-84 ms.
- **Undo AI turn** stays under 5 ms on both paths at every size.

On a phone at 4-6× slower, the worst live call at 5,000 is roughly 250-370 ms, a noticeable
hitch once per call but not an unresponsive app. The ceiling is not lowered.
