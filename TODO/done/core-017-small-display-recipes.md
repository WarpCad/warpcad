> ## What actually shipped (retirement note)
>
> All four recipes, standalone, plus a shared standalone `Hat` and a shared expansion
> kit. Every part of all four matches **real V1 output** to 9 decimal places.
>
> ### 1. The nested-addressing question, answered — and the answer is "no, for a reason nobody expected"
>
> This file's Proposed fix opened by asking whether a composite can reference one of its
> own nested records' sub-parts. It was probed directly against `kernel/index.js` rather
> than reasoned about, and the answer has three parts:
>
> - **Sibling references by bare id work fine inside a composite's own fold.**
>   `core-020`'s isolation fix walls an expansion off from the *caller's* `initial`
>   state, not from itself — the nested `evaluate` threads one shared `byId` through its
>   own records exactly like the document fold does.
> - **`{ id, pieces }` addressing also resolves inside that nested fold** — but only for
>   `group.snap`/`group.align`. `index.js:147-159` rejects a `pieces` operand on any
>   entry that is not `rawOperands`, and `modify.bisect` is a plain `native(1, …)`. **So
>   the restriction is an op-level one, identical at document level and inside a
>   composite. It is not a nesting limitation, and no `kernel/index.js` change would
>   lift it.** That is a different answer from the one this file's framing expected, and
>   it is the reason the fallback was taken rather than a fold change attempted.
> - **Referencing a bisect group by bare id is worse than the error**: `toGeom3`
>   combines `negative` and `positive` back together, so a second cut would silently
>   receive the whole uncut slab. The fold's explicit throw is what prevents that, and
>   it is right to throw.
>
> **Path taken: the direct-construction fallback, and it is exact rather than
> approximate.** `group.js`'s `bisect` does not cut against a plane — it builds one
> axis-aligned box covering the half-space past the cut and subtracts or intersects with
> it. Keeping the `negative` half *is* that subtraction. So the chained bisects port as
> `boolean.subtract` against the same half-space boxes `bisect` itself would have built
> (`halfSpaceCutter`), giving bit-identical geometry with no new addressing.
>
> **Therefore no `kernel/index.js` fold change was made, and `core-020`'s finding is not
> on this task's regression surface on the grounds this file names.** It was re-run
> anyway, on the separate and stronger grounds that this task adds four new *composite
> kinds* to the expansion path — the same grounds `core-014`/`015`/`016` each used.
> Result: for all four, the composite's group holds only its own parts, the incremental
> path matches the batch path exactly, and `boolean.subtract(plate, screen)` yields a
> real plate rather than the EMPTY solid the bug produced. Full detail in
> `TODO/ACCEPTANCE.md` scenario 1.
>
> ### 2. The board is not an L, and this file's other geometric miss is bigger
>
> **A V1 execution fixture was built, reversing this file's "No V1 execution fixture"
> call.** This file asked for that judgement to be made independently rather than copied
> from `core-015`/`016`, so: the deciding factor is that **four** of the behaviours these
> four recipes depend on are readable only from the `jscad-utils` *implementation*, and
> two of them are mis-stated in this file's own Proposed fix. A hand-written assertion
> for any of them restates the reading it is meant to check. The fixture
> (`__fixtures__/raspberrypi.smallDisplays.v1-reference.json`) was generated by
> `core-014`'s procedure. Unlike `core-016`'s, it needs no lens-relative trick: every V1
> factory here is called with **no board**, and each one's own `if (piMotherBoard)` /
> `if (rpi)` guard then skips the attachment translate, so V1's own standalone output is
> directly comparable. It passed on the first run, so it was **mutation-tested** — all
> three mutations tried are caught loudly (details in §6).
>
> **(a) `miniPiTFT`'s board is not an L-shape.** This file describes the two chained
> bisects as cutting "an L-shaped notch". They do not: both cuts are straight and each
> removes a whole slab, so the result is a plain 38.1 × 24.384 rectangle whose two *left*
> corners are still rounded — the lower one truncated by the y cut — and whose two right
> corners are square. The L is in how the cuts are *specified*, not in the board. The
> spec asserts this by corner-volume shortfall, derived from the 32-gon the kernel
> actually builds, because both bounds checks would also pass for a plain cuboid.
>
> **(b) `display-clearance` is a frustum, not a box — this file's step 4 reads only half
> of the chain.** Step 4 says `miniPiTFT.js:125` is `.stretch('z', 5)` and asks for "one
> correctly-sized `primitive.cuboid`". The actual line is
> `g.parts.display.stretch('z', 5).chamfer(-5, 'z+')`, and the **negative** chamfer is
> not decoration: `util.chamfer` builds its far slice as
> `enlarge(slice, [-radius * 2, -radius * 2])` and `util.enlarge` adds an absolute delta
> to each dimension, so a negative radius **flares the face outward**. Following step 4
> literally produces a clearance volume 10 mm too small in x and in y — for a clearance
> volume that is a functional error, not a cosmetic one. See §4 for what it cost.
>
> **The `stretch` half of step 4 is confirmed, and stated as confirmed rather than
> guessed**, exactly as this file asks. `stretchAtPlane`'s semantics are: cut the solid
> at a plane, insert `distance` of material, translate one side. The target here is a
> plain, feature-less cuboid this same recipe built nine lines earlier, with nothing
> inside it for the inserted material to interact with — so on this box, inserting 5 mm
> at a cut plane is bit-for-bit the same solid as building the box 5 mm taller. The
> stretched size is computed analytically and one `primitive.cuboid` emitted. **Checked
> at this call site specifically**, which is the only `stretch` in these four recipes;
> the equivalence holds because the target is a just-built plain box and must be
> re-checked, not assumed, at any future call site.
>
> **`Parts.Tube`'s argument order is confirmed**, as step 3 asks:
> `Tube(outsideDiameter, insideDiameter, height)` (`jscad-utils/src/parts.js:371`) takes
> **diameters**, matching `primitive.tube`'s `diameter`/`innerDiameter`/`height` exactly.
> No adaptation. Step 5 is confirmed too: `Parts.Cylinder(4, 3).fillet(1, 'z+')` is
> `modify.filletRim`'s own signature unchanged.
>
> ### 3. Two V1 behaviours the fixture caught that no reading would have
>
> **`util.calcmidlineTo` is a *relative* translate.** It returns `to - size / 2` and
> never consults where the solid currently is (`jscad-utils/src/util.js:874`). At almost
> every call site a preceding `snap(…, 'inside-')` puts the solid's minimum on its
> reference's minimum, which makes it *look* absolute. `miniPiTFT`'s buttons are the
> exception in this task: they are snapped to a board whose y minimum is **1.016**, not
> 0, so the midline lands 1.016 mm further out than the name suggests. Ported as the
> relative translate it really is.
>
> **`PiTFT24`'s `buttonWire` variable is never reassigned.** `:153` builds it and snaps
> it in x and z but not y; `:175` adds `buttonWire.snap(connector, 'y', 'inside-')` — a
> *new* solid — to the group while the local still refers to the un-snapped one; `:195`
> and `:212` then build the clearance from that local. So the clearance is positioned
> from the wire's pre-snap y, not from the wire the group actually contains, and reaches
> to y = 1.45 instead of 0.95 in the default case. Reproduced, not corrected: the port
> keeps a `buttonWire.proto` part and a placed clone, mirroring V1's two solids.
>
> ### 4. Scope grew in three places, all stated
>
> **(a) `src/core/kernel/fillet.js` — outside this file's Affected files.**
> `chamferRim`/`filletRim` rejected every negative radius (`requireRadius`: `value <= 0`
> throws), so V1's outward flare (§2b) was not expressible. Rather than approximate a
> clearance volume away, the op was taught V1's own semantics: `Math.abs(radius)` sets
> the band height and the cut plane (exactly as V1's `sliceParams` does), the sign drives
> the offset direction, and `assertRadiusFits` — whose whole purpose is catching an
> *inset* that collapses the outline through zero — is skipped for an outward offset,
> which has no such failure mode.
>
> **That exposed a real, pre-existing bug in the rim sweep.** `expansions.offset` returns
> an **outward** outline with its vertex list cyclically rotated by one
> (`[[13,-13],[13,13],[-13,13],[-13,-13]]` against a base of
> `[[-10,-10],[10,-10],[10,10],[-10,10]]`), and `extrudeFromSlices` walls slice to slice
> *by index* — so every wall quad joined a corner to its neighbour and the loft came out
> twisted. A 3 mm flare on a 20 mm cube measured 556 mm³ where 1596 mm³ was right, and
> crept toward the true value as slices were added rather than being wrong by a fixed
> amount, which is what a twist looks like rather than a rounding error. Fixed by
> `alignVertices`, which picks the cyclic shift minimising total squared distance between
> the two rings. **Shift zero wins for every inward offset**, so it is a provable no-op
> for everything that worked before — every existing `fillet.spec.js` analytic-volume
> test still passes to its original tolerance, and a fillet driven through the live UI
> measures 63 700.84 mm³ against `git show HEAD:…`'s 63 700.904 mm³. A count mismatch
> between the two rings now throws by name instead of lofting nonsense.
>
> **One residual inaccuracy, measured and documented rather than papered over**:
> `sectionAt` reads a cross-section through `project`, whose `unionGeom2` round-trip
> inflates the outline by a shape-dependent ~6e-6 to 6e-5 mm. On an inward rim that lands
> inside the remainder and never shows; on an outward flare the loft *is* the bounding
> box, so it leaks out. `display-clearance` is therefore compared to V1 at 4 decimal
> places rather than 9 — 8 nanometres on a 35 mm volume — and the tolerance carries the
> reason inline. Pre-existing, not introduced here.
>
> **(b) `src/core/kernel/recipes/raspberrypi.hat.js` (new).** See §5.
>
> **(c) `src/core/kernel/recipes/rpi-recipe-kit.js` (new).** Five expansions (four
> recipes plus the Hat) needed the same ~200 lines of probe-and-bake helpers.
> **This is deliberately *not* the extraction `core-016` deferred**: nothing in
> `raspberrypi.bplus.js`, `cameraModule.js`, `touchDisplay7inch.js` or `touchScreen2.js`
> imports from it or was touched. That refactor is still open and still wants its own
> task with the existing fixtures as its safety net; this is the module it should grow
> into rather than a sixth copy. What is genuinely new here, and what the deferred task
> did not have to solve: `booleanPart`/`modifyPart` apply ops *inside* an expansion to
> parts the expansion just placed, and an operand that is also a named part in its own
> right (V1 does this constantly) is emitted exactly once.
>
> ### 5. `core-018` / `Hat` ordering — the flag this file asked for
>
> `PiTFT22`/`PiTFT24` never touch `piMotherBoard`; they forward it into
> `Hat(piMotherBoard, options)`, which uses it only for its own attachment translate. So
> dropping it means calling a **standalone Hat**, which is what V1 itself does when the
> argument is omitted. `core-018` had not landed, so per this file's instruction the
> expansion was built here — as a shared module, `recipes/raspberrypi.hat.js`, not
> inline in one recipe.
>
> **`core-018` should register that module rather than re-port `hat.js` from scratch.**
> Its header carries the four-line wrapper needed to turn `addHat` into a
> `'raspberrypi.hat': composite(0, hat)` entry, and `HAT_MM`'s hole table is there for
> the `hat-standoff.js` port to reuse — note those holes sit at 3.56 mm on a
> 65.02 × 56.39 board and are **not** `MOUNTING_HOLES_MM`'s B+ positions.
>
> ### 6. Smaller notes
>
> - **`PiTFT24`'s spacer bars are measured, not recomputed**, as this file requires: the
>   four spacers are built and placed for real, `calcSnap` measures the gap between two
>   of them, and the bar length is baked from that. The spec asserts the emitted bars
>   against the emitted spacers' real bounding boxes, so deriving the length from
>   `HAT_MM` a second time would still pass today but could drift silently later.
> - **`buttonWireYOffset`'s two branches are both tested**, plus a negative offset, which
>   has no V1 fixture and is asserted as the documented displacement from the
>   fixture-backed zero case.
> - **`group.holes` is not expressible** (no `OpRecord` field reaches `group.js`'s
>   `addHole`), so the Hat's four mounting holes are hidden named parts and the boards
>   render undrilled — `core-014`'s deviation 2, unchanged. For `PiTFT22` these are an
>   *addition* to V1's own part dictionary, which keeps them only in `holes`; emitting
>   nothing would have lost them.
> - **V1's duplicate leaf entries are collapsed** where the combined entry and its leaf
>   are the same solid (`buttonCap1`/`buttonCap1-cap`; `mb`/`hatmb`; `gpio`/`hatgpio`) —
>   `core-016`'s precedent. Where the union is genuinely more than either leaf
>   (`button1` = base ∪ push) all three are emitted, as V1 has them.
> - **`hyper-pixel-4-rectangular.js:23-24`'s first x snap is dead code** — `inside-`
>   immediately overwritten by `inside+` on the same axis — and is not reproduced. Both
>   calls being on the same axis is what makes that safe to state rather than guess.
> - **Mutation-tested** (the fixture passed first time, so this is what says it can
>   fail): making `midlineTo` absolute fails `miniPiTFT`'s `button1 min[1]` by exactly
>   1.016 mm, the board's own y minimum — the single call site in these four recipes
>   where the quirk is observable; flipping the chamfer radius positive fails two
>   `miniPiTFT` tests; measuring `PiTFT24`'s wire clearance from the placed wire instead
>   of the prototype fails the fixture diff.
> - **A pre-existing `ui-027` limit surfaced while driving Step 6, not a regression**:
>   a second rim op on a *perpendicular* face of an already-filleted body is refused by
>   `assertPrismaticBand`, which `ui-027`'s Interaction model says should work. It
>   reproduces identically against `fillet.js` at HEAD. Logged in `TODO/VERIFY.md`.
> - **No `docs/` page and no `*.e2e.js`**, as this file specifies: none of the four has a
>   UI surface. `src/core/library.js` filters the Library panel to `primitive.*`, which
>   was re-confirmed live — the panel still renders exactly seven rows.


## Goal

Four composite recipes reproducing `jscad-raspberrypi@62a8e3b`'s `miniPiTFT(options,
piMotherBoard)`, `PiTFT22(options, piMotherBoard)`, `PiTFT24(options, piMotherBoard)`,
and `HyperPixel4Rectangular(rpi)`. All four take an optional external board parameter
per `docs/decisions/composite-recipe-geometry.md`'s Family B table — **drop it and
expand standalone**, same requirement `core-016` implements for the two touch
displays, with the attachment becoming the user's own `group.align`/`group.snap`
(`ui-026`). `PiTFT22`/`PiTFT24` additionally **forward** their board parameter straight
into `Hat(piMotherBoard, options)` (`pi-tft22.js:29`, `pi-tft24.js:61`) rather than
using it directly, so "drop the parameter" here specifically means calling this task's
own standalone `Hat` expansion (from `core-018`) with no board, not stripping a
forwarded call that never touches the board itself.

**This task also needs one thing none of `core-014`-`016` did: `modify.bisect` and
`modify.fit`/`enlarge`/`filletRim`/`chamferRim` used *inside* a composite's own nested
expansion, not just as a document-level operator a user applies afterward** — V1's
`miniPiTFT` board is carved from a rounded rectangle by two chained `.bisect()` calls
(`miniPiTFT.js:90-93`), and its buttons use `.fillet()`/`.chamfer()`/`.enlarge()`
internally too. See Proposed fix for whether that composes cleanly with the fold as it
exists today.

## Proposed fix

**First, confirm whether a composite recipe can reference one of its own nested
records' sub-parts as the operand to a later record in the same expansion.**
`registry.js:762-767`'s own comment states `modify.bisect` "[r]eturns a two-part
`Group` (`negative`, `positive`)", and `ui-016`'s retirement note already established
that its pick-depth mechanism should reach into a bisect result "with no further
change once this task appends a record" — but that was stated for a *document-level*
record, not for a second record inside the same composite's own nested fold array
referencing the first by `{ id, pieces: 'positive' }`. `core-013`'s `{ id, pieces }`
addressing (`registry.js:673-713`) was built and tested for `group.snap`/`group.align`
between two *document* bodies; whether the same addressing resolves against a sibling
record still inside one composite's own nested array needs a real check against
`kernel/index.js`'s fold (`core-020`'s fix already established that a composite's
nested fold is isolated — confirm addressing *within* that isolated fold works the
same way, not assume it does). If it does not resolve cleanly, the fallback is
computing the bisect's board shape analytically instead (see next paragraph) rather
than blocking this task on a fold change.

**`miniPiTFT`'s board — the one genuinely new geometric question in this recipe.**
`Parts.RoundedCube(inch(2), inch(1), 2, 2).bisect('x', inch(1.5)).parts.negative
.bisect('y', -inch(0.96)).parts.positive` (`miniPiTFT.js:90-93`) cuts an L-shaped notch
out of a rounded rectangle with two straight cuts. `modify.bisect`'s registered form
(`registry.js:765-767`, `group.js`'s `bisect(value, axis, offsetMm)`) is a single-
operand op over one solid — chaining two of them is exactly what a document-level
tool already supports (two consecutive `modify.bisect` operations, each keeping one
half), so this reduces to: build the rounded-rectangle profile+extrude
(`kit.board.js`'s pattern), one `modify.bisect` record on it keeping `.negative`, a
second `modify.bisect` record on *that* keeping `.positive` — resolved either as two
nested-composite records (if the check above confirms it) or, failing that, as one
directly-computed rounded shape (the L-shape's four corners are all analytically
knowable from the two cut offsets, so an equivalent `profile.roundedRectangle`-minus-
rectangle construction is a safe fallback that needs no new addressing at all).

**`miniPiTFT`'s buttons use `Parts.Tube` (`buttonCap`, `standoff`,
`miniPiTFT.js:177`) — confirm `jscad-utils`' `Parts.Tube(outer, inner, height)`
argument order against this project's `primitive.tube`'s `diameter`/`innerDiameter`/
`height` params (`registry.js:461-479`) before porting; don't assume radius-vs-
diameter matches without checking, the same caution `core-010`'s fit-table diffing
already modeled for a different primitive.**

**`.stretch()` and `.enlarge()` calls decompose without a new kernel op.** Both
`miniPiTFT.js:125` (`display-clearance`, `.stretch('z', 5)`) and
`camera-module-v2.js`'s `lensribbon` (`core-015`, for cross-reference) apply
`jscad-utils`' `stretch` (`~/src/jscad/jscad-utils/src/util.js:1314-1324`, wrapping
`@jscad/csg`'s `stretchAtPlane`) to a **plain, feature-less cuboid the same recipe just
built** — confirmed by reading `stretchAtPlane`'s own semantics (cut the solid at a
plane, insert `distance` of material, translate one side): on a box with no internal
geometry, inserting material at a cut plane is bit-for-bit equivalent to building the
box at its already-stretched final dimension in the first place. So `display-
clearance` needs no new `modify.stretch` op — compute the stretched size analytically
(here, `z` height + 5mm) and emit one correctly-sized `primitive.cuboid` directly, with
its position derived the same probe-and-bake way as every other part. **State this as
a confirmed simplification in the retirement note**, not a guess — the equivalence
holds only because every V1 `.stretch()` call site in this catalogue targets a
just-built plain box, and that should be verified against each call site it's applied
to (also present in `core-015`'s `lensribbon`), not assumed to generalise beyond this
catalogue.

**`buttonCap`'s fillet/chamfer usage maps directly to `modify.filletRim`/
`chamferRim`** (`registry.js:720-733`) — `Parts.Cylinder(4, 3).fillet(1, 'z+')`
(`miniPiTFT.js:44`) is exactly that op's own signature (radius, face orientation), no
adaptation needed.

**`PiTFT22`/`PiTFT24` both call `Hat(piMotherBoard, options)`** — this task depends on
`core-018`'s standalone `Hat` expansion existing (or, if scheduled first, this task
should expand `Hat` inline and `core-018` should be the one to extract it — flag the
ordering decision in whichever task lands second rather than duplicating the port).
**`PiTFT24`'s spacer/gusset assembly (`pi-tft24.js:236-309`) is the most complex single
piece of geometry in this task**: four cylinder-minus-bolt-hole spacers positioned at
the Hat's own four mounting holes, connected by two X-axis and two Y-axis connector
bars whose length is *computed from the spacers' own measured separation*
(`util.calcSnap(spacerGroup.parts.spacer1, spacerGroup.parts.spacer2, 'x', 'inside+')`)
— a textbook case for the probe-and-bake pattern (build all four spacers for real,
measure the gaps, bake the bar lengths), not an attempt to derive the separation from
the Hat's hole-position constants a second time.

**`HyperPixel4Rectangular`'s board is itself a `boolean.subtract`** (`board.subtract
(notch)`, `hyper-pixel-4-rectangular.js:27`) followed by a whole-group
`transform.rotate` (`group.rotate('mb', 'z', 180)`, `:55`) applied *before* the
optional board-relative attachment step is dropped — port the rotate as an ordinary
internal record; it is not part of the Family-B attachment being removed.

## Effort

L — `PiTFT24`'s spacer/gusset assembly and `miniPiTFT`'s double-bisect board are real
geometric-design questions (not wiring), and the nested-composite sub-part-addressing
question above needs a real answer, possibly a small `kernel/index.js` fold change if

direct-construction fallback.

## Testing

Vitest, one `.spec.js` per recipe, plain Node. No V1 execution fixture. Geometric-
relation assertions: `miniPiTFT`'s board bounding box matches the two bisect cuts'
resulting L-shape dimensions; `PiTFT24`'s four spacer centres match the Hat's own
mounting-hole positions and the two connector-bar lengths match the *measured* spacer
separation (asserted against the real bounding boxes, mirroring `kit.anchor.spec.js`'s
own "assert the baked offset against the real measurement" convention, not against a
recomputed constant); `HyperPixel4`'s board bounding box is unchanged by the 180°
rotation (a symmetric-enough check) but the notch cut is confirmed present by volume.
Degenerate cases: `PiTFT24`'s `buttonWireYOffset` has a documented `<= 0` branch
(`:163,204`) — test both branches explicitly, not just the default.

No `*.e2e.js` — no UI surface exists yet.

Acceptance scenarios: none directly pass/fail on this task alone. If the nested-
composite addressing question above requires a `kernel/index.js` fold change, that
change extends the same fold `core-020`'s closed finding was filed against (composite
expansion isolation) — re-run `core-020`'s original repro (an incrementally-appended
composite must not absorb an earlier body into its own group) against the changed
fold before calling this task done, per `CLAUDE.md`'s standing-regression-surface
rule.
